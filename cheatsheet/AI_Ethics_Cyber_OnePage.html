0. Basics: Bias vs Fairness U2★★

Bias = systematic error → unfair decisions (a.k.a. ML/algorithmic bias); sources: data collection, algorithm design, human bias. Fairness = correcting algorithmic bias so outcomes are just & equitable w.r.t. sensitive/protected attributes (race, gender, religion, age, SES).

Types of bias (8): Sampling · Algorithmic · Confirmation · Measurement · Generative · Reporting · Automation (trust machine over human) · Group-attribution (in-group, out-group homogeneity). Pipeline view: data (historical, representation, measurement) → modeling (evaluation, aggregation) → human review.

Types of fairness (5): Group (groups treated equally) · Individual (similar people → similar outcome) · Procedural (fair, transparent process) · Counterfactual (same result if attribute flipped) · Causal (no decisions from historical inequality).

Principles: Non-discrimination, Equitable treatment, Transparency, Accountability, Ethical considerations.

Impact — fair AI: trust, equal opportunity, legal compliance, social cohesion, business benefit. Biased AI: reinforces inequity, loss of trust, discrimination (loans/jobs), legal risk, economic & social cost.

1. Techniques for Measuring Fairness & Bias U2★★★

  1. Disparate Impact Analysis – does AI hurt one group more? Procedure: group fairness, statistical tests, thresholds. Used in hiring, lending, insurance.
  2. Fairness Through Awareness – fairness metrics built into training/eval: Demographic Parity, Equal Opportunity, Equalized Odds.
  3. Counterfactual Fairness – same outcome if sensitive attribute changed (individual fairness, counterfactuals).
  4. Explainability & Interpretability – feature importance, local explanations (LIME/SHAP).
  5. Bias Detection Tools – bias metrics + auditing tools: AI Fairness 360 (IBM), What-If Tool (Google).
  6. Human-in-the-Loop – expert judgment, feedback loops.
  7. Ethical Impact Assessment – stakeholder consultation, risk assessment, mitigation strategies.

2. Techniques for Mitigating Bias U2★★★

Pre-processing → In-processing → Interpretability → Post-processing
  1. Data collection & pre-processing: diverse & representative data; bias detection (statistical parity, disparate impact); augmentation, anonymization, careful sampling, reweighing.
  2. Algorithmic fairness (in-processing): fairness constraints on sensitive attributes; fairness-aware algorithms optimizing equal opportunity / equalized odds; regularization.
  3. Interpretability & transparency: XAI; regular bias audits.
  4. Post-processing: reweighing, demographic parity adjustment, disparate impact remover, threshold adjustment — applied after training.

7-step holistic approach: Data & preprocessing → Algorithm design → Fairness-aware training → Evaluation/testing (bias audits) → Transparency/XAI → Monitoring & accountability → Policy, regulation & governance.

6. Generative AI U2★

AI that learns patterns from data and generates new content (text, image, video, music, 3D). Driven by LLMs / foundation models.

Works in 3 phases: ① Training → foundation model on huge data ② Tuning → fine-tuning (labeled task data) / RLHF (humans score outputs) ③ Generation, evaluation, re-tuning (+ RAG = retrieve external docs → generate; current & verifiable).

Architectures: VAE (compress features → variations; 2013) · GAN (generator vs discriminator; 2014) · Diffusion (remove noise step-by-step; DALL·E) · Transformer (attention; "Attention Is All You Need" 2017; basis of GPT).

Key concepts: generative models · latent space (hidden representation; sample → diverse outputs) · backpropagation (find where error came from) · gradient descent (reduce error).

Timeline: ELIZA 1964 → GPU (GeForce 256) 1999 → Autocomplete 2004 (Markov chain) → VAE 2013 → GAN 2014 → Transformer 2017 → GPT-2/3 2019-20 → ChatGPT 2022 → Gemini, Copilot, Llama, watsonx.

Applications: creative arts, content/marketing, healthcare (synthetic images), gaming/VR, personalization. Advantages: creativity, faster decisions, dynamic personalization, 24×7.

Risks: hallucination (fix: guardrails, RAG) · inconsistent output (prompt engineering) · bias · black box (XAI) · security/privacy/IP · deepfakes. GenAI vs AI: creates new content vs predicts/classifies.

8. Importance of Privacy & Security in AI M3★★

  • Protect personal info → prevents identity theft, fraud
  • Build trust → users adopt trusted AI
  • Regulatory compliance → GDPR/HIPAA; avoid fines
  • Ethical responsibility → no misuse of data
  • Prevent discrimination → anonymize, reduce bias
  • Model integrity → defend against AI threats: data poisoning, adversarial examples, model inversion, membership inference, model theft, prompt injection

AI + Data ⇒ Privacy (protect) + Ethics (use responsibly) + Fairness (avoid unfair outcomes)

CIA triad: Confidentiality · Integrity · Availability. Privacy by design, data minimisation, encryption, access control.

11. PPDM – Privacy-Preserving Data Mining M3★★★

Extract useful patterns while hiding sensitive info.

  • Anonymization (generalization/suppression; Age 23 → 20-25, Noida → NCR, names removed):
    • k-anonymity: each record indistinguishable from ≥ k−1 others (“who is this?”)
    • l-diversity: ≥ l distinct sensitive values per group (fixes homogeneity attack)
    • t-closeness: group's sensitive distribution within t of overall (fixes skew attack)
  • Data perturbation / randomization: add noise/swap values (20,21,22,23 → 20,22,21,24), patterns kept
  • Cryptographic: encryption, HE, SMPC for distributed (horizontally/vertically partitioned) data
  • Rule hiding: suppress sensitive association rules
PPMLPPDM
protect privacy while learning/training models (FL, DP, HE, SMPC)protect privacy while mining patterns (anonymize, perturb, crypto)

Strength: k < l < t. Trade-off: more privacy = less data utility.

12. Risk Management: Assessment & Incident Response M3★★★

Risk Assessment – identify, evaluate, prioritize risks

  1. Asset & threat identification: hackers, malware, insiders, disasters, AI attacks
  2. Vulnerability assessment: bugs, misconfigs, human error, weak models
  3. Impact analysis: operations, reputation, finance, assets
  4. Risk evaluation: Risk = Likelihood × Impact (Severity) → risk matrix (High/Med/Low)
  5. Mitigation / treatment: technical controls, policy, training. Options: Avoid · Reduce · Transfer (insurance) · Accept
  6. Monitor & review continuously

Incident Response (NIST SP 800-61 lifecycle)

① Preparation → ② Detection & Analysis → ③ Containment, Eradication & Recovery → ④ Post-Incident Activity (lessons learned) ↺

Preparation = IR plan, team (CSIRT), drills/tools. Detection = SIEM/IDS alerts, triage. Containment = isolate systems; Eradication = remove malware/root cause; Recovery = restore from backup, verify. Post-incident = review, report, update plan.

Goal: minimize damage, restore operations quickly, prevent recurrence. AI frameworks: NIST AI RMF (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage).

14. Ethics Across the AI Lifecycle M3★★

Problem definition (stakeholders, purpose, impact assessment) → Data collection (privacy GDPR/CCPA, informed consent, diverse data) → Model development (transparency, bias mitigation, accountability) → Evaluation (fairness across groups, adversarial robustness, explainability) → Deployment (monitoring, user training, feedback) → Operation & maintenance (ongoing evaluation, model updates, ethical audits).

15. Case Studies: Implementing AI Ethics in Engineering M3★★

Google AI Principles (2018): socially beneficial, avoid unfair bias, safe, accountable, privacy, scientific excellence. Implementation: bias audits, diverse data, safety testing, AI ethics review board. Outcome: more trust, transparency, accountability, less bias.

IBM AI Fairness 360: open-source toolkit, 70+ metrics & mitigation algorithms (reweighing, adversarial debiasing, reject-option). Microsoft Responsible AI: fairness, reliability & safety, privacy & security, inclusiveness, transparency, accountability.

Failures (lessons): Amazon hiring tool (penalised women – trained on male-dominated CVs; scrapped) · COMPAS (recidivism scores biased vs Black defendants) · facial recognition higher error on darker-skinned women.

Other frameworks: PAVED (Partnership on AI – transparency, fairness, accountability) · IEEE Global Initiative – Ethically Aligned Design · EU AI Act (risk-based).

Best-practice checklist: ethics review → DPIA/impact assessment → diverse data & documentation (datasheets, model cards) → fairness testing → XAI → security testing (red-team) → human oversight → monitoring & audits.
